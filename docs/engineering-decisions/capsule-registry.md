# Capsule registry contract (NEAR testnet; ledger entry for the NEAR loop, acceptance I-08 and III-08 stay BLOCKED on the live key)

## Target experience
1. A judge or recipient holding a capsule can ask the chain three things: who registered this digest and when, does that account control the key that signed it, and did a model enclave sign each receipt in it.
2. References: SealedBounty `contracts/sealed-bounty/src/tee.rs` (MIT, 7a60122, READ IN FULL: signed-text rebuild and `env::ed25519_verify`; also reads the `eth` and `gateway` kinds and a verdict parser, none of which apply here). Antibody (MIT, e155d3d) `contracts/near-antibody/src/tee.rs` (266 lines): READ IN FULL; its `lib.rs` (1382 lines) NOT READ. NEAR docs `smart-contracts/anatomy/environment` (host functions), `testing/integration-test`.
3. Approach: no owner, no admin, no upgrade; first registration wins; the binding signature names the calling account; storage is billed to the caller and the surplus returned.
4. Criteria: replay by another account fails; a forged or altered receipt fails on the real runtime; a weak key cannot forge.

## Found by running it on a real runtime
- The unit-test mock accepted nothing odd, but the sandbox node ACCEPTED an all-zero public key with an all-zero signature as valid (a small-order key, the classic universal forgery). Any contract that checks Ed25519 on chain with an unvetted key can be fooled. Fixed: `is_small_order` rejects the seven y-values of every point of order 1, 2, 4 and 8 (sign bit ignored, non-canonical forms included), for receipts and for the capsule signer.
- The mutant that removes this guard SURVIVES the unit tests (the mock does not show the flaw) and is CAUGHT only by the sandbox test. So the sandbox test is part of the gate for this contract.

## Evidence
- 18 unit tests (rg01 to rg18) and 1 sandbox test on a local NEAR node (VERIFIED by run): registration, replay by another account, forged receipt, weak signer, billing under 0.2 NEAR for 1 attached with the rest refunded, first registration wins, the free `verify_receipts` view.
- Mutants: 10 of 10 on the registration and receipt rules caught; 5 more on the weak-key rule, 4 caught by unit tests and 1 (receipts skip the guard) caught only by the sandbox test.
- Wasm 135.5 KB built with `cargo near build non-reproducible-wasm` (sha256 b4e0a8f1...9155 for this source; the reproducible build needs Docker and was not run).

## Audit 1 change (see audit-1.md)
- Entries are now keyed by digest plus signer and the binding message covers a digest of the receipts (`kakurega-register-v2`), closing a squatting hole an independent reviewer found: before, anyone could register a capsule's digest first with their own key and keep the owner from registering it. The registrant still proves control of A key only; whether it is the capsule's signer is the verifier's check. Unit tests rg01 to rg18 plus rg05b and rg05c, sandbox run on a real runtime including a squatting and a swapped-receipts case, 3 new mutants caught. The wasm was NOT rebuilt for a deploy (nothing is deployed).

## On-chain hardware check: the open question is answered
- Can a NEAR contract check a TDX quote? Yes: Antibody (MIT, e155d3d) `contracts/near-antibody/src/lib.rs` lines 861 to 894 calls `verify_quote` on a DCAP verifier contract with the quote and its collateral, at about 176 Tgas on testnet by its own measurement (line 45). Read by me: those lines only; the rest of `lib.rs` is still NOT READ. The NEAR docs search (`near-docs` MCP, query "DCAP verifier contract verify TDX quote on chain") returned no page for it; that is one search, not proof the docs lack it.
- No top-ranked privacy entry does this for NEAR AI Cloud receipts (Aftercall, Veto, TEE Receipts and cipherwork all trust a signer key their owner registered). Building it here would let the registry say, on chain, that a receipt's signer key is bound into a quote Intel vouched for.
- OWED before building: read the rest of Antibody's `lib.rs` and `tee.rs` verification path, find the verifier contract's source and account, check that NEAR AI Cloud's quote and collateral are in the form it accepts, and decide how approved images are named without an owner.

## Experiment: NEAR's on-chain verifier accepts a real NEAR AI Cloud model quote (VERIFIED by run, no key, no transaction)
- The verifier is `near/mpc` `crates/tee-verifier` (MIT; `src/lib.rs` read in full, `tee-verifier-interface/src/lib.rs` read for the types): stateless, no admin, `verify_quote(quote, collateral)` in Borsh, returns the verified report or a rejection as a value. Deployed at `tee-verifier-2026-07-22.testnet` (account and code hash read from the testnet RPC).
- What was run: a model attestation for `Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B` fetched from `cloud-api.near.ai/v1/attestation/report` with a nonce chosen here; Intel's collateral fetched from Intel PCS for the quote's platform (FMSPC read from the quote's certificate); `verify_quote` called as a view on testnet.
- Result: verified, TCB status `UpToDate`, no advisories, a TD 1.0 report. The report data's first 32 bytes equal the model's Ed25519 signing key and its last 32 bytes equal the nonce chosen here. `mr_config_id` begins with `01` followed by 32 bytes (the SDK's rule: the SHA-256 of the app compose).
- So a contract can establish on chain, with Intel's roots and no trusted registrar: this key is bound into a quote from genuine TDX hardware at this patch level, for a challenge the caller chose. The same key is the one whose signature the registry already checks on each receipt.
- Sizes: quote 5,006 bytes, call arguments 19,223 bytes. Gas for a real call was not measured here (Antibody measured about 176 Tgas for its quotes).
- Not shown by this: NVIDIA GPU evidence (checked off chain only), the RTMR3 event-log replay, which images are approved (no owner, so the contract records measurements and judges none).

## Built: the hardware check on chain (`attest_signer`, `hardware`, `challenge_for`)
- Target experience: a recipient asks the chain "was this receipt's signing key inside genuine Intel TDX hardware, at what patch level, and who asked?", with Intel's roots and NEAR's own verifier, and no registrar anyone has to trust.
- Sources read by me: `near/mpc` `crates/tee-verifier/src/lib.rs` (in full) and `crates/tee-verifier-interface/src/lib.rs` (the types; MIT, mirrored field for field in `src/hardware.rs`, credit in NOTICE.md); Antibody (MIT, e155d3d) `contracts/near-antibody/src/tee.rs` in full and `lib.rs` 845 to 935 (the call and its callback), `crates/cli/src/tee.rs` 14 to 34, 84 to 125 and 200 to 242 (collateral from Intel PCS); the NEAR AI SDK `src/bindings.rs` (the report-data layouts). NOT READ: the rest of Antibody's `lib.rs` and CLI, `tee-verifier-conversions`, `dcap-qvl` itself.
- Decisions: ADAPT the call shape and the result-as-a-value handling from Antibody; EXCLUDE its image allowlist and vetoes (they need an owner; this contract records the measurements and approves none); BUILD the challenge rule (`sha256("kakurega-attest-v1:<account>")` must be the quote's second half, so a quote requested by one account cannot be recorded by another), the weak-key refusal, storage billing with the rest returned, and a callback that never panics (a panic there would keep the deposit).
- A receipt registered after its signer was attested carries `signer_attested_at`; one registered before, or by an unattested key, carries none.
- Evidence: 35 unit tests (hw01 to hw14 with hw08b; hw01 decodes the real verifier answer saved from testnet). Mutants: 20 planted across `hardware.rs` and the callback; 18 caught at first, 2 survived (a refusal that returned nothing but did not refund) and are caught since hw08b asserts the reason and the refund on every refusal.
- LIVE on a real runtime (`cargo test --test sandbox live_ -- --ignored`, VERIFIED by run): a local NEAR node, the verifier's real code fetched from testnet, a fresh `Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B` quote from NEAR AI Cloud requested with the caller's challenge and no key, Intel's collateral fetched live. A quote with one changed byte: rejected by the real verifier, nothing recorded, deposit returned. The same quote relayed by another account: nothing recorded. The caller's own quote: recorded with `UpToDate`, the model's key, the measurements and the compose hash. Cost to the caller about 0.026 NEAR of 0.1 attached.
- Limits, to be stated wherever this is shown: a recorded key says the key was in TDX hardware when the quote was made and that Intel's collateral accepted it at the block time; it does not say the quote is recent beyond that, which software ran (measurements are recorded, not judged), or anything about the GPU. The verifier account is fixed at creation; whoever controls that account's code is trusted (NEAR's verifier has no admin by its own source, which is bound to its code hash).
- Not done yet: testnet deployment, the client command that fetches the quote and collateral, the verify page reading `hardware`, gas measured on testnet, the reproducible build.

## Orders: paid on a verified capsule (built; local node only for now)
- Target experience: a client sets money aside for one review and it is released by the chain, not by either party's say-so, when the named reviewer registers a capsule for that order, signed by the key the client expected and, if asked, carrying only model receipts whose signer's hardware quote this contract saw verified after the order opened. An unmet order returns to the client after its deadline.
- Strongest reference read: Aftercall (MIT, a367ec7) `contracts/agent-wallet/src/lib.rs` lines 376 to 398 (token receiver), 660 to 782 (escrow open, release, confirm, return) and 853 to 878 (the release callback). There the payee's confirmation releases the money; here the release condition is evidence the contract itself checks. NOT READ: the rest of that file (caps, payees, cross-chain payouts).
- Approach: ADAPT one accepted token fixed at creation and the callback that puts a failed transfer's state back; BUILD the order-bound registration (the capsule key signs the usual binding message plus `:order:<id>`, so a signature for one order or for a plain registration cannot be used for another), the hardware requirement, the earned state with a retry anyone may call, and refusal of token transfers that carry no valid terms (the tokens are handed back by the token standard's own refund).
- Criteria: nobody but the reviewer is paid; no late, replayed or wrongly signed capsule is paid; nothing is sent twice; a failed transfer never loses the money or the record.
- Evidence: unit tests od01 to od14 (49 in the file with the earlier ones), each refusal asserted by its reason and each case on its own thread, because the test runtime keeps storage across cases and a first version of these tests passed for the wrong reason. 31 of 31 planted faults caught. On a real local node (`orders_pay_the_reviewer...`, VERIFIED by run): the order's amount is the deposit less its storage; another account is refused with the reviewer's signature and with its own key; the reviewer is paid in the registering transaction; the paid order cannot be claimed or refunded; a second order cannot be refunded before its deadline, cannot be earned after it, and returns to the client.
- What the chain does not decide: whether the review is good, and whether the capsule's own scope matches the order's `scope_sha256` (recorded for the recipient's verifier to compare).
- The token path on a real local node, with wrapped NEAR's real contract code fetched from testnet (`live_a_token_order...`, VERIFIED by run): a transfer with no terms and one below the smallest order come back in full; a transfer with terms opens an order in the token; a reviewer who cannot hold the token yet leaves the order earned with the money in place; after she registers with the token, a retry by anyone pays her once.

## The fee, and the deployment nobody can change (VERIFIED by run)
- Decision (the owner left pricing to me): the workspace is free; Kakurega earns 2% on top of the reviewer's amount on each order that is paid. Comparison, with sources in the README: Immunefi and Sherlock 10% on bounty payouts, Upwork about 5% to clients plus a fee to freelancers, Escrow.com about 0.9% to 3.25%, Code4rena 0%. No human triage or dispute handling is offered here, so the rate sits with escrow, not with marketplaces.
- Contract: the rate (at most 10%) and the one fee account are fixed at creation. A client may name the reviewer's amount; then exactly its price is kept and the rest handed back (in NEAR by a transfer, in the token by the token's own refund). The fee is earned when the capsule is registered, returned with the amount on a refund, and sent to the fee account by `withdraw_fees`, which anyone may call; the balance is taken off before the transfer and put back if it fails, and the callback never panics (docs.near.org/smart-contracts/security/cross-contract-calls/reentrancy, callback-panics, refunds).
- Evidence: unit tests fe01 to fe11 (61 in the file); 11 planted faults in the fee code all caught (one found a weak test, which was fixed). Real local node: `a_fee_on_top_is_paid...` (NEAR: price kept, rest back, reviewer paid the whole amount, fee to the fee account by the client's call, refund returns the amount and the fee) and the token test extended (price kept, rest refunded by the token, a withdrawal to an account that cannot hold the token leaves the fee owed and held, then succeeds).
- Independent review of the fee change (a separate reviewer given the code and the specification, 205k tokens, read-only): no critical or high finding; the fee is never paid twice or to the wrong account. Every finding was checked in the code and was real, and all are fixed: (medium) a token order's record and a paid order's growth were stored at the registry's expense, so a client recycling 1 USDC through 60-second orders could drain the free balance until payouts failed (docs.near.org/smart-contracts/security/storage: users cover the state they add) — a token order is now paid from its client's storage balance (`storage_deposit`, `storage_balance_of`, `storage_withdraw`, NEP-145 style; without one the transfer comes back whole) and the growth of a paid order is charged with the registration; (low) a named amount on the token path was split again and drifted by one unit — stored exactly as priced; `new` was not private — now `#[private]`, so only the deploying transaction can create it; change that could not be sent back was stranded — it becomes the payer's withdrawable storage balance; a misspelt order field was ignored — unknown fields are refused; the token minimum was checked on what was sent — now on the reviewer's amount; the fee account could be the registry or the token — refused; an order paid through another contract refunded to that contract — an order can name its `client`; the open event lacked the amount and fee — added; the chain client signed whatever price an RPC reported — it now checks the amount and that the fee is within the 10% cap before signing; the quote's text was misleading when the registry could not be read — it now always names the reviewer's amount and the client, uses the registry's price or 2%, and says so.
- Evidence for the fixes: unit tests fe12 to fe21 (72 in the file); 10 new planted faults caught, and the 11 earlier ones still caught; real local node: a token transfer with terms and no storage balance comes back whole and opens nothing, the order's record comes out of the client's balance, a fee sent to an account that does not exist stays owed, another account calling `new` on undeployed-init code is refused ("private").

## Every receipt checked, a compact record (VERIFIED by run)
- Found with the first real NEAR review: its capsule carried 20 model receipts and a registration took at most 16, so the chain checked 16 of 20.
- Decision: verify up to 128 receipts per registration and keep their digest (the text the binding signature already covers), their count and the distinct model keys with how many receipts each signed and when its hardware was recorded, instead of each receipt. Storage no longer grows with the number of receipts (an entry of 128 is under 600 bytes); the hardware rule for orders reads the keys. Readers (`kaku chain capsule`, the public verify page) re-compute the digest from the capsule's first `receipt_count` receipts, which are the ones a registration carries, and say so when it differs; a registry of the earlier kind is still read receipt by receipt.
- Evidence: unit tests rg30 (128 receipts, digest, signer counts, size) and rg31 (one bad signature among 40 refuses the whole registration), 74 in the file; 4 of 4 planted faults caught (only the first 16 checked, no duplicate check, the hardware rule skipped, a wrong count). Real local node: 128 receipts registered in 44.3 Tgas for under 0.02 NEAR of storage and gas.
