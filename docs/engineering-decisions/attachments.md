# Attached files (ledger G-GH-12)

## Target experience
A person drops a contract, a spreadsheet or a PDF into the chat, sees exactly what was read from it and where, and asks about it. Nothing leaves the computer until they send, a stranger's file cannot give the assistant orders, a secret in it never goes to a model that is not on this computer, and a file that cannot be read says why instead of failing silently.

## References and reading depth
- AnythingLLM collector (MIT, commit eda0eb5): FULL read of `processSingleFile/index.js`, `convert/asTxt.js`, `asXlsx.js`, `asDocx.js`; `utils/files/index.js` `isTextType` (read for the text-detection idea). `convert/asPDF/index.js` and `asPDF/PDFLoader/index.js` FULL (read later, commit eda0eb5): pdf.js text items per page, a newline where the vertical position changes, empty pages skipped, and when a PDF has no text at all it falls back to an OCR loader. NOT READ: `asEPub`, `asMbox`, `asImage`, `asAudio`, `utils/OCRLoader`.
- Discovery from that reading: the OCR fallback for text-less PDFs is the one capability of the reference that we say we lack. It is recorded as the owed gap in the next section (a local OCR engine is a large dependency and a licence decision; it needs a read of `OCRLoader` and of tesseract-class engines first). Its line grouping by vertical position is what lopdf's extractor also does; our PDF fixtures show the same line breaks.
- Docling (MIT), by our own run X8 (`offline-parsing.md`): found 95 of 95 seeded items on the same files, including table cells with row and column. NOT used as the engine: it needs Python and 1.3 GB of models, so it cannot be a default part of an installed program.
- Decision per capability: REBUILD the readers in Rust with MIT crates for the container formats (zip, quick-xml, lopdf) and our own walkers; take the text-decoding order and the ISO-date and exact-number rules from AnythingLLM as ideas (no code); EXCLUDE OCR and speech for now (said to the owner, never faked).

## What was built
- `crates/kk-parse`: text (UTF-8, UTF-8 with BOM, UTF-16 with BOM, Windows-1252; 40-line blocks anchored `lines A-B`), web pages (visible text only), Word (paragraphs, tables with `table T, row R, column C`, headers, footers, footnotes, endnotes, comments), Excel (every non-empty cell with its address, blocks of 25 rows anchored `Sheet!A1:C25`, dates as ISO, exact long numbers, formulas as their last value), PDF (text layer per page, anchored `page N`), archives (members read one by one, anchored `archive > member > place`), pictures (named, not read).
- What the owner sees is what is read: hidden text, deleted tracked changes, hidden sheets, rows and columns, hidden page elements and comments are left out and counted in plain words.
- Hard limits: 8 MiB file, 400,000 characters out, 16 MiB per unpacked part, 48 MiB unpacked in all, 200 files per archive, 2 levels of archives, 400 PDF pages, 40 sheets, 200,000 cells, a ratio of 200 between packed and unpacked parts, XML nested at most 256 deep, no custom entity ever expanded.
- The service never parses a file itself in production: `kaku parse-attachment` is started as a separate process with no environment, a 30 second limit and a 12 MiB output cap; bytes in on standard input, one JSON line out.
- `POST /api/attachments?name=` (raw bytes), `GET /api/attachments/{id}` (what was read, line by line, with each line that would be left out marked), `POST /api/attachments/{id}/remove`. Files live in memory only, at most 12 and 3,000,000 characters at once, for two hours. The log records that a file was read (kind, size, characters), never its name or text.
- Chat: `attachments: [ids]` (at most 5) go to the model as one labelled block of DATA with the bracketed place of each passage. Lines that read like instructions (the shared injection reader) are always left out; lines holding a secret are left out when the model does not run on this computer. Only the offending lines go, except an order split over several lines, which takes its whole passage. The answer reports files read, cut to fit, left out, and the parts withheld. A kept conversation records `[attached: name]`, never contents.
- Web: Attach files button, drop onto the message box, a card per file (kind, size, characters, notes, flags), What was read (each line, struck through when left out), Remove.

## Evidence
- kk-parse: fixtures from independent tools (python-docx, openpyxl, reportlab): the same 95 truth items as X8, each found with the right anchor (fx01); hostile and edge cases kp01–kp20 (size, names, encodings, hidden text in web pages and Word, entity attacks, nesting, Excel dates and hidden parts, compression bombs by size, ratio, entries, total and depth, paths in archives, password-protected PDF and zip, scans, 540 deterministic mutations of the fixtures without a panic or hang); 33 mutants on the safeguards, all caught after assertions were added; the PDF empty-password call was removed as dead (the library decrypts on load).
- kk-service: attachments unit at01–at11; flow sv96–sv102 (upload and read back of every kind, refusals with reasons, labelled data to the model, remote-model secret withholding, kept conversation records names only, limits, window budget).
- kk-cli: pc01–pc06 (a hung, crashing, flooding or lying reader costs one attachment; the reader gets no environment) and pa01–pa03 against the real binary.
- Browser: attach, refusals, flags, What was read, send, drop, Remove, five-file limit (e2e 296/296).

## Not covered, said plainly
- Text recognition exists on macOS only (see the next section). On other systems, or when the helper is not installed, scanned PDFs and pictures are named, not read. NOT VERIFIED on a photographed paper scan (fixtures are drawn text, rotated, blurred and speckled).
- PDF text comes from the text layer in reading order; table structure (rows and columns) is not recovered, so a PDF table cell is anchored to its page only. Docling does better here. Text hidden in a PDF (white or tiny text) is not distinguished.
- The reader process has no operating-system sandbox profile (no network deny, no memory cap) beyond the time, output and environment limits; it has no network code path. A Seatbelt or seccomp profile is owed.
- Old `.doc`, `.xls`, `.ppt`, OpenDocument, EPUB, mbox, audio and video are not read (they are refused as an unsupported kind).
- Extraction quality on large real-world documents beyond the 95 seeded items was not measured. A hostile PDF that exploits the parser itself is bounded by the process limits, not proven harmless.

## Dropping and pasting files in the web chat (ledger B-W25)
- Read in full: OpenCode `packages/app/src/composer/dropzone.tsx` (92 lines) and `attachments/attachments.ts` (360 lines), commit fe04656, MIT. Not read: `uploads.ts`, `destination.ts`, `draft-stash.ts`. What it does: document-level dragover/dragleave/drop listeners with a full-composer overlay; a drop target only for drags that carry `Files` (or a workspace file link); Escape and leaving the window clear it; paste handling for clipboard files and images; duplicate detection by content id plus filename (clipboard images by bytes alone); a MIME check that normalises text-like files; uploads that finish if the composer unmounts and that sending waits for.
- What we already had (built earlier, see above): choosing files, a drop on the message box, chips that say what each file is, the kernel's per-line withholding, limits of 5 files and 8 MB, and What was read.
- Gaps found by comparing, and now closed: (1) a file dropped anywhere outside the message box made the browser open it in place of the page, losing the draft: the page now takes a file drop anywhere and cancels the browser's own handling; (2) the drop target flickered on moving over the box's children and showed for dragged text: it now shows only for drags that carry files, clears on Escape and on leaving the window, and says "Drop to attach"; (3) no paste of files: a file in the clipboard is attached and pasted text is left to the browser; (4) the same file could be attached twice: a SHA-256 of the bytes plus the name identifies it (size and modified time where the browser cannot hash), the page says "already attached", and removing a file lets it be attached again.
- Differences from the reference, on purpose: no inlining of images (they are not read by this program), no staging by path (the bytes go to the local service as they are), no upload cancel button (a read is quick and bounded by 8 MB).
- Evidence: web unit tests at10–at12, the browser suite (11 new checks including a drop outside the box, the same file twice, paste of a file and of text, Escape, re-attaching after Remove), a screenshot of the drop target read by eye, 6 mutants caught after two checks were added. Hindi strings for the three new texts are written by me and unread by a person (HUMAN.md).
- Not covered: dragging a file out of a workspace tree (the reference's `@mention` drop), a drop while a reply is streaming (ignored, as the picker is disabled then), touch devices, a screen-reader announcement of the drop target beyond its `status` role.

## Text recognition for pictures and scanned pages (ledger G-GH-12)
- Target: a person drops a photographed contract or a scanned PDF and gets what a text file would get: the words, where they came from, injected orders left out, secrets withheld, all on this computer.
- Reference reading: AnythingLLM `OCRLoader` (tesseract.js, MIT) and the matrix row were the starting point; the decision differs on purpose. Reading depth for `OCRLoader`: NOT READ in full (the engine choice rests on the next point, not on its code). Engines compared by running, not by name: tesseract and ocrmypdf are NOT installed here; Apple's Vision framework was run on a rotated, blurred, speckled picture and read it exactly (OBSERVED, helper tests pa04-pa06). Vision is already part of the operating system, so it adds no dependency, no model download (Docling needs 1.3 GB) and no licence to ship; it never sends a picture anywhere.
- Built: `helpers/ocr/kaku-ocr.swift` (Vision for pictures, PDFKit renders pages to pictures; at most 20 pages, 50 million pixels, a 12 MiB input; answers one JSON line). `crates/kk-cli/src/ocr.rs` starts it from the reader process with no environment, under a Seatbelt profile that denies network and file writes (the profile is tested against a local listener, with an unconfined control, and against a write), with a 45 second limit and a 4 MiB output cap, and does not wait on the pipe once the helper is killed. `crates/kk-parse` takes the engine as a parameter (`parse_with`), so the crate stays free of processes: a picture (also one inside an archive) and every PDF page with no text layer are recognised, labelled `recognised text` or `page N (recognised)`, with a note that the text may contain mistakes, kept in page order next to the pages that have a text layer. Without the helper or the sandbox tool the old honest note stays.
- Recognised text is untrusted like any other: it goes through the same cleaning, the character budget, the instruction reader and the secret check as every other passage. In the browser a picture that says "ignore all previous instructions" is flagged before anything is sent, and one holding a key is flagged as a secret.
- Found by running: Vision read the digit 7 inside the well-known documentation example of an AWS access key as the letter Z. Recognition can change characters, so a secret in a picture is detected as recognised; a token whose shape the engine breaks could escape the check. Said here, not hidden.
- Found and fixed on the way: when a program the reader starts is killed, a grandchild can keep the output pipe open and the reader waited for it (30 seconds in the test). Both the new runner and the existing attachment runner now stop waiting at once (pc02b, oc02b fail on the old code).
- Evidence: kk-parse rc01-rc08 (stand-in engine: labels, failures, silence, pages not asked for, cleaning, the budget, a picture in an archive); kk-cli oc01-oc06 (stand-in helper: good answer, hang, held pipe, crash, junk, flood, missing, no environment, error codes, confinement by default) and the Seatbelt profile test; pa04-pa10 against the real helper and real reader (rotated speckled JPEG, image-only PDF, text page plus scanned page in both orders, a 20000 by 20000 picture bomb, a broken picture, no helper); browser: four files attached, flags and passages checked, axe on the page, screenshot read by eye. 13 mutants of the new safeguards, all caught (one only after the network test got a local listener and a control).
- Not covered: Linux and Windows (no engine; named, not read); packaging the helper inside the desktop app and the installer; handwriting; languages other than the system's default for Vision; a photographed paper scan; tables in a scan (text only, no rows and columns); a scan with several hundred pages (cut at 20 pages, said in the note); the helper running as a user with a different Vision language setting.

## Pictures and scans through the real web page with the real model (OBSERVED, ledger G-GH-12)
- Run: the real service with qwen2.5:3b and the operating system's text recognition; a real browser (Playwright) opened the pairing link, attached a file in the chat, asked, and read the answer. Three files, one conversation: a rotated, speckled picture of an invoice; a PDF with a text page and a scanned page; a picture that tells the assistant to ignore its rules and send a key.
- The invoice picture: the answer was exactly right (total 1,280.00 EUR, signed by Priya Natarajan for Acme Audit Ltd), 3.5 s, and the card said the text was recognised by this computer and may contain mistakes. The mixed PDF: the facts were right (delivery on 14 March, 2 percent a week) but the 3B model hedged and in one run said it could not see a second page. The injected picture: both instruction lines were left out and counted ("2 part(s) of the files left out because they read like instructions"); the model never received an order.
- A weakness seen once: after the invoice question, asked to summarise the injected picture (all of whose text was withheld), the 3B model described an invoice, from the earlier turn, instead of saying it had nothing. Repeated five times each way: with the old text the model said "not provided with this message" in 5 of 5; with an added sentence telling it plainly that nothing of the file was given and not to guess, it did so in 3 of 5, twice quoted my sentence back as if it were a rule it must obey, and once recited the invoice again. **The sentence did not help and may confuse a small model, so it was not kept** (reverted before any commit). The once-seen fabrication is NOT fixed; its rate is low in these runs and unmeasured beyond them.
- Not covered: a stronger model, a longer conversation, more than one withheld file at once, scans of real paper.
