# Experts, roundtable, lead and activity (ledger G-GH-33 and G-GH-37 to G-GH-41)

## Target experience
1. The owner asks one question and gets several independent expert answers side by side, then one resolution that says where they agree, where they differ and what to do; or hands a larger job to a lead that splits it, follows each part, and stops for the owner whenever something leaves the computer or changes a file. At any time one screen shows what is running, waiting, blocked or stalled, and nothing is lost when the service restarts.
2. Strongest references: OpenCode (child sessions, jobs and recovery), Hermes (roundtable, result caps, the task board), goose (approval forwarding from a child, required final output), OpenCode's Claude agent provider (an outside coding agent that can only use the host's tools). Ghast's team mode is the experience benchmark (user side only).
3. Approach: build on the one kernel. A participant is never a second authority: it answers through the same model runner and, when it acts, through the same broker with facts no wider than its parent's. Roundtable first (it needs no new power), then the lead and handoffs on the existing job records, then the activity view, then Claude Code as a teammate through `kaku mcp`.
4. Quality criteria: a participant sees nothing its route may not carry; one participant's failure never loses the others' answers; every answer that comes back is capped and framed as untrusted text; a child can never do what its parent could not; a restart loses no finished work; the owner can stop everything at once.

## Decisions
| Capability | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Roster of experts | BUILD our own, texts written here; an expert is a name, a group, an instruction and optionally one of the installed models. Owner-added experts are approved like methods (content pinned by hash) | Ghast's texts are closed; OpenCode's roster carries permissions, which here belong to the kernel alone |
| Roundtable | ADAPT Hermes's shape (independent answers, labelled failures, kept order, synthesis with a raw fallback); IMPROVE: each participant's route is checked against the data's label before it sees anything, answers are capped and framed as untrusted before the lead reads them, the resolution has fixed parts (agreed, disputed, decision, open questions), a stop reaches every running call | the references send one conversation to every provider and cannot stop a call |
| Result cap | ADAPT Hermes's rule (a share of the reader's remaining window, with a floor and a ceiling, head and tail kept); the full text stays in the vault, not in a readable spill file | their spill file is deliberately not private |
| Lead, handoffs, completion conditions | BUILD on the existing job and effect records: a task graph whose parts each name an expected result; completion is the kernel's check where one exists (a reproducer, a test run), the owner's say otherwise. OWED reading first: Hermes kanban | "non-empty text" is their completion evidence |
| Child permissions | BUILD: a child is another worker session on the same broker with facts no wider than its parent's; nothing is inherited by name, nothing is saved as "always allow" across sessions | the gap read in OpenCode's rule resolution |
| Activity view | BUILD on the run and job records, with stale detection and a warning before a cut; OWED reading: OpenCode restart, Hermes dispatch | |
| Claude Code as a teammate | ADAPT OpenCode's approach: start it with its own tools off and only `kaku mcp` allowed, so each of its actions is a Kakurega tool call with the existing leases and approvals. ## Built: the roster and the roundtable (G-GH-37, G-GH-38)
- `crates/kk-service/src/experts.rs`: twelve built-in experts in four groups (texts written here), the lead's instruction, the cap for one answer in the lead's prompt (a share of the model's window between 1,500 and 6,000 characters, beginning and end kept on line boundaries), the block an answer is put in for the lead (an answer cannot close its block or open another), and the reader of a resolution.
- `GET /api/experts` (names and one line each, never the instructions) and `POST /api/chat/roundtable` (2 to 5 experts, each once): every expert gets exactly what an ordinary chat would have sent for the question (the same notes, files, methods and rules, on the same route) plus its own instruction placed after the product's; no expert sees another's answer; an expert that fails or says nothing is listed as missing and the rest stand; the lead is asked only when at least one answered; if the lead fails the answers are still returned; closing the page stops the answer being written and asks no one else. In a kept conversation the question and the resolution are saved, not the experts' own words. The signed log records counts only.
- Web: a Roundtable screen (grouped picker with a limit, live answers per expert, the resolution with the decision first, the choice remembered on the browser). Terminal: `kaku roundtable QUESTION --experts ID,ID [--json]` and `--list`.
- Found with the real local model (qwen2.5:3b): the lead left out a part it had nothing to say under, so the first reader (all four parts required) showed a perfectly usable resolution as "unstructured". The reader now requires the decision, takes the other parts when present, once and in order, and shows a part that was left out as left out. The lead is also told to write every heading.
- Evidence: `kk-service` ex01 to ex06 and sv190 to sv194 (what each expert saw and did not see, the lead's prompt with a hostile expert answer that tries to close its block and write a decision, caps, missing experts, a failing lead, a lead that rambles, nobody answering, a kept conversation, stopping); 13 of 14 mutants caught, the fourteenth equivalent (after the page closes the next "asking" line cannot be sent, which already ends the loop). `tests/e2e/roundtable_web.mjs`: 20 checks against the real service with the scripted model, and again with the real local model (exit 0, the resolution in its four parts; screenshots read by me, desktop and phone in dark mode). `tests/e2e/script_cli.py`: 6 checks of the terminal command.
- Limits: every participant is the same model on the same route, so a roundtable is several points of view, not several independent minds, and the page says the decision is advice, not a check. Expert names are in English in the Hindi interface. An answer the model stopped writing at its own length limit is not marked as cut. With several routes (after the NEAR key) each participant's route must be checked against the data's label before it sees anything; today there is one route, so the chat's own rule covers it.
- Owner's own experts: an approved method can be asked as an expert (`method:NAME`), with its approved text in the frame every method gets. One that is saved but not approved, or edited after approval, is not offered and is refused before any model is asked (sv233; browser checks "an approved method is offered under its own heading" and "it answers at the table like any expert"). The roundtable tests are sv228 to sv233 (renumbered; sv190 to sv194 belonged to the methods tests).
- Full-screen terminal: `/roundtable EXPERT,EXPERT : QUESTION` shows one card per expert that fills as the answer arrives, then the resolution; alone it lists the experts and the owner's approved methods; Esc twice stops it. Like an aside it sends no conversation id, so nothing is saved, and its cards are left out of what is copied, searched and exported. In plain-line mode an answer is read out only when whole. Evidence: tu112 (events, hostile characters, a stray event, refusals, stop, the service going away), `tests/e2e/tui_pty.py` (2 checks in the real pseudo-terminal, screen read by me), `tests/e2e/tui_linear.py` (5 checks).
- OWED in this group: the lead with handoffs and completion conditions, the activity view, Claude Code as a teammate.
